# Comparable apps: what made them grow (researched 2026-10-25)

Rule: describe competitors by what they do, never claim they're dying.

## Airbuds (music social, closest cousin)

- 15M+ downloads, 5M monthly / 1.5M daily users (TechCrunch, Sept 2025);
  mostly US high-schoolers and college students. $10M raised.
- **Hook:** an iOS home-screen widget showing what friends are streaming.
  "Effortless. You just connect your Spotify." Founder: asking users to
  build a playlist is "a lot of effort". Streaming apps "gave us access to
  100 million songs, but nobody really cracked the identity piece."
- Reactions (emoji, stickers, cut-out selfies), chat, "ghost mode",
  taste-similar friends, **Weekly Recap** (Wrapped-style), profile
  "Spaces", a school feature in testing.
- **Invite-gating:** see more than your top 3 artists in the recap only
  after inviting friends.
- **TikTok volume:** reportedly 50+ accounts ("firstname.airbuds") posting
  2–20 lo-fi videos a day each; a person, a text hook, a trending sound;
  ~10 minutes per video; student ambassadors paid weekly; creators
  without a viral video in their trial get dropped. Hooks are shared
  across creators. Examples: "What the Airbuds reactions are starting to
  feel like"; "Me dodging the question because my favorite Taylor song is a
  very unpopular opinion". (Vendor blog: treat numbers as unverified.)

**For Peak Music:** low-effort identity beats high-effort tasks. The
showcases, taste match and share cards are the identity layer, so keep
making them richer and more postable. An iOS widget (e.g. your Song of the
Day, or a friend's latest rating) is a real future option but needs a
native build plus App Review (widgets can't show ads: Apple 2.5.18). The
ambassador model is the scalable version of Luca's "no face" rule:
someone else's face, his hooks.

## Letterboxd (the model Luca already cites)

- ~1.7M users (2020) → 26–30M (2026); half under 35, 16–24 the biggest
  group. Valued for **no algorithm feed, no aggressive ads**, community
  first. Owner reportedly exploring a sale (2026); monetization has been
  the weak spot.
- Money: free tier with ads; **Pro** (no ads, stats, filters) and
  **Patron** (everything + custom posters/backdrops, Patrons directory
  listing, early features), sold on web and in the app stores. December
  2025: a film rental store.
- Growth: diary/log culture, reviews written as jokes that get screenshot
  to TikTok, year-in-review, studio and festival partnerships, community
  members as red-carpet "critics".

**For Peak Music:** the supporter tier pattern (Patron = recognition +
cosmetic perks) is exactly Luca's donator-checkmark idea (see
web-monetization skill). The "funny one-line review screenshot" is a
proven organic format: a weekly "best one-liners on Peak Music" carousel
(with permission/credit) costs nothing.

## Spotify Wrapped

- 2020: 90M+ engaged, +21% app downloads in the first week of December.
  2025: ~200M engaged, ~500M shares in 24 hours (agency figures).
- Why: personal, a little flattering or funny, a distinct visual every
  year, and a share button on every card. Each year spawns imitators.

## Receiptify (the one-person viral tool)

- A student project: your top Spotify tracks as a shop receipt. 1M+ uses in
  the first months, spread on Twitter and Instagram Stories.
- Why: a nostalgic, instantly readable format; pick a time window; one PNG
  to post. Imitators (Icebergify, Festify, Instafest) followed.

**For Peak Music:** new share-card *formats* are cheap marketing. Ideas
grounded in data the site has: "my 0–10 ratings as a receipt" (careful:
not borrowing Receiptify's exact look), "my hottest takes" (biggest gaps
from the community average), "albums I rated 10". Each new card = a reason
to post again.

## Strava

- Kudos (9.6B in 2021) and clubs make solo effort social; people in groups
  log 80–125% more activity. **Year in Sport** is a yearly earned-media and
  reactivation spike. Subscriptions are ~90% of revenue.

**For Peak Music:** kudos = likes on reviews (exists). The Strava lesson is
that the *group* drives volume: Aux Wars rooms and release-night rooms are
the "group run". Push people into them on release days.

## Pattern across all of them

1. A **low-effort core action** (connect Spotify / log a film / record a
   run). At Peak Music: rate with the slider, ~10 seconds.
2. An **identity layer** people want to show (widget, profile, stats).
3. A **share artifact** that looks good out of context.
4. A **seasonal moment** (Wrapped, Year in Sport, Year in Review).
5. **Social density** before fancy features.
